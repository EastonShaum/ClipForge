# Account ramp-up: TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, X (2026)

Researched 2026-10-26. Every source line shows **(published or "undated" / accessed 2026-10-26)**. Anything published before 2026-04-26 is marked **⚠ possibly stale**. **Folk wisdom** means creator or vendor advice that the platform has not confirmed.

## TL;DR

- **No platform has an official "warm-up" rule.** The 5–7 days of scrolling before you post is folk wisdom. It's cheap insurance for a brand-new account, so do a short version: about 3 days of normal use, then post.
- **Originality is the real risk in 2026, not the warm-up.** TikTok's For You rules exclude reused content "with nothing new added" (effective 2026-09-24). Instagram stopped recommending accounts that mostly repost others' work (2026-04-30). YouTube cut Shorts reach for channels that mostly re-upload others' clips (2026-10-01). Every clip needs your own captions, hook text, edits and framing, and no other app's watermark.
- **Disclose every paid campaign clip.** Use the platform toggle (TikTok "Disclose commercial content", YouTube "paid promotion", Instagram "Paid partnership", X "Paid Partnership") **and** put "#ad" or "Paid partnership" text on screen. The FTC says the platform tool alone may not be enough.
- **One person, one phone, one account per platform is fine and allowed.** Every platform permits multiple accounts, but don't build several accounts that post the same thing. X explicitly bans duplicate-purpose accounts, and making a new account to dodge a ban is banned everywhere.
- **Cadence for days 1–14:** about 1 post/day per platform in week 1, rising to 2/day on TikTok and Shorts in week 2 if nothing is flagged. Check each app's account-status page every day.

## 2. Warm-up behavior

**None of this comes from an official platform document.** I found no TikTok, YouTube, Meta or X page that tells new accounts to warm up. All of it is folk wisdom, mostly from vendors who sell account tools:

- Spend a few days only watching, then add likes, follows and comments, and post last. Keep activity steady and avoid sudden spikes ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/tiktok-account-warm-up/), undated, folk wisdom).
- Use the account normally for about 5 days without posting, then make one test post ([Superscale](https://superscale.ai/learn/the-right-way-to-warm-up-new-tiktok-instagram-accounts/), undated, folk wisdom).
- Post directly in the TikTok app for the first 7 days rather than through a scheduler ([OneUp help](https://help.oneupapp.io/en-us/article/why-are-my-tiktok-videos-getting-0-views-m13bwc/), undated, folk wisdom). This doesn't matter much for you because you post manually anyway.

**Why do it anyway?** It costs almost nothing. Watching and engaging inside your niche also tells TikTok and Instagram what your account is about, which matches how TikTok's official explanation says recommendations use interactions. Keep it short (about 3 days) and don't overthink it.

**Do on every platform:**
- Fill in the full profile: picture, bio that says what you post and "clips with permission" or similar, and a niche-matching name (see `account-names.md`).
- Verify your phone number and email.
- Watch and engage with **your niche only**: gaming clips, podcast clips, and the creators you'll clip.
- Make **Instagram a Professional (Creator) account.** Clipping marketplaces like Whop need this to connect ([Opus summary of Whop docs](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards), 2026, third-party), and Instagram's branded content tools need it too.

**Don't, on any platform:**
- Buy followers, likes or views, or use engagement "pods". This is banned explicitly: TikTok bans "the trade of services that artificially boost engagement" ([TikTok Community Guidelines, Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity), effective 2026-09-24, accessed 2026-10-26). Campaign platforms also don't pay for botted views ([Whop docs via Opus](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards)).
- Mass-follow or mass-like in bursts, or use automation scripts. TikTok bans automation that gets around platform systems (same TikTok source), and X bans automation and mass registration ([X platform manipulation policy](https://help.x.com/rules-and-policies/platform-manipulation), page dated March 2023 per search snippet **⚠ possibly stale**).
- Upload a clip that still has another app's watermark on it (see section 4).

## 4. Shadowbans and "unoriginal / reused content" flags

"Shadowban" isn't an official term. In practice it means your content is made ineligible for recommendation feeds while it stays on your profile. Every platform now does this openly, and each has a way to check.

### TikTok
- **Rule:** "Content with unoriginal or reused material and nothing new added" is ineligible for the For You feed (FYF) ([TikTok Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity), effective 2026-09-24, accessed 2026-10-26; FYF framework at [FYF standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards), released 2026-08-25).
- **Account-wide penalty:** if you repeatedly post FYF-ineligible content, your **whole account** can become temporarily ineligible for recommendation and harder to find in search. TikTok notifies you and you can appeal. Check your standing under **Settings → Account → Account status / Account Check**, which reviews your last 30 posts ([TikTok Newsroom CG update](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/cgupdate?lang=en-ZA), 2024-04-22, **⚠ possibly stale**; the UI may have moved).
- **Undisclosed commercial content:** if TikTok flags a post as commercial (payment, a brand tag or logo, product recommendations) and you didn't disclose it, you get a notice and **24 hours** to fix it before the video loses FYF eligibility. TikTok says proper disclosure does **not** hurt recommendations ([TikTok Ads Help, content disclosure for creators](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-the-content-disclosure-setting-for-creators), updated April 2026, accessed 2026-10-26, **⚠ possibly stale**, right at the 6-month line).
- Folk wisdom: other platforms' watermarks and static or near-unedited reposts are the most common triggers ([Online Optimism](https://onlineoptimism.com/blog/updates-tiktoks-community-guidelines), undated).

### YouTube Shorts
- **Reused content (monetization policy):** this covers channels that "repurpose content that's already on YouTube or another online source". It's judged **separately from copyright**, so having the creator's permission does **not** exempt you. **Allowed:** clips in a critical review, reactions that comment on the original, sports replays where you explain the moves, and others' footage edited with your own storyline and commentary. **Not allowed:** moments edited together with little or no narrative, short videos compiled from other social sites, and non-verbal reactions with no voice commentary ([YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en), last listed change 2025-07-15, **⚠ possibly stale**).
- **Inauthentic content (renamed 2025-07-15):** templated, mass-produced, "interchangeable" videos are ineligible for monetization (same page).
- **NEW, 2026-10-01: lower Shorts reach for re-uploads.** YouTube said Shorts recommendations "give further priority to original material" and cut distribution for channels that mainly repost others' videos without significant changes. Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie said "VO descriptions of what's happening on screen, minor technical edits, or template-based bulk changes" don't count as original. Commentary, analysis and storytelling do ([Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/), 2026-10-01; YouTube's video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XS2A6aExLaA). **Whether permission-based clipping is treated differently is not stated.** There's also no dashboard telling you if your reach was cut ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-original-content-update/), 2026-10-03).
- This applies only if you ever apply for YouTube monetization (YPP). Campaign pay doesn't depend on YPP, but recommendation reach does depend on the 2026-10-01 change.

### Instagram Reels
- **2026-04-30:** accounts that repeatedly repost content they didn't create are no longer eligible for recommendations (Explore, Reels feed to non-followers). This already applied to Reels and now covers photos and carousels too. **Original** means you made it, or you **materially edited** third-party content (unique text, creative edits, voiceover, commentary). **Not original:** adding a watermark, changing speed, or credit tags alone ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/), 2026-04-30; [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/), 2026-04-30). One report says accounts with licensing or resharing agreements are exempt, but I couldn't confirm that from Meta's own wording.
- **Watermarks:** Instagram has made Reels with other apps' logos or watermarks (such as TikTok's) "less discoverable" since 2021 ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/02/10/instagram-reels-with-tiktok-watermark-less-discoverable/), 2021-02-10, **⚠ possibly stale**, but it fits the 2026 rules).
- Check **Settings → Account Status** for "recommendation eligibility" ([Sociallyin](https://sociallyin.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-update-repost/), 2026, relaying Mosseri).

### X
- X's spam policy targets duplicate or coordinated multi-account activity and automation ([X platform manipulation policy](https://help.x.com/rules-and-policies/platform-manipulation), March 2023 **⚠ possibly stale**; the page blocked direct fetch, so this is from the search snippet).
- X tightened rules against copying third-party content to qualify for its *creator revenue sharing* ([wwwhatsnew on Nikita Bier's tenure](https://wwwhatsnew.com/2026/08/08/nikita-bier-deja-x-twitter-head-product-agosto-2026/), 2026-08-08). That only matters if you apply for X revenue share.

### How clipping, commentary and transformative edits fit these rules
1. **Permission isn't originality.** The campaign gives you the *right* to use the footage, which deals with copyright. The platforms' originality systems ask a different question: did *you* add something? That's explicit on YouTube (reused content is "separate from copyright"), and Instagram says credit doesn't count.
2. **What counts as "adding something", using each platform's own examples:** your own hook text or headline, context captions ("Why this mattered:"), your own storyline across cuts (setup → payoff), zooms and reframes that serve the story, commentary or voiceover that adds *opinion or analysis* (not just narrating what's on screen; YouTube said that by name), and choosing and tightening the moment.
3. **What doesn't count:** speed changes, mirroring, borders, a watermark, a credit tag, auto-captions alone, the same template on every clip, or reposting another clipper's cut.
4. **Never upload a file downloaded from TikTok or Instagram with its watermark.** Export a clean file from your editor and upload *that* to each platform.
5. **Folk wisdom:** campaigns may reject clips that look like another clipper's post from the same source ([OpenClip](https://openclip.app/clipping/avoiding-duplicate-content-flags.md), vendor, undated). Pick different moments from everyone else.

| X | Up to **10 accounts** for **"different, non-duplicative purposes"**. **Device and IP:** no platform publishes a "one account per device" rule. Vendor blogs (mostly proxy sellers) claim TikTok links accounts by device ID and IP, and that more than 3 accounts on one phone can be flagged ([Proxyway](https://proxyway.com/guides/how-to-manage-multiple-tiktok-accounts), [RecurPost](https://recurpost.com/blog/multiple-tiktok-accounts), undated). That's **folk wisdom from interested parties.**

**For you (one phone):**
- One account per platform on one phone is completely normal. Logging into TikTok, YouTube, Instagram and X on the same phone is not a "multi-account" issue, because they're different companies.
- If you later add a **second TikTok account** (for example, gaming and podcasts split), 2 on one phone is commonly done. Keep their content clearly different, and never post the same clip to both. Don't use VPNs or proxies to "separate" accounts; that's the behavior spam systems are built to catch (folk wisdom, but low cost to follow).
- If an account gets banned, **do not** make a replacement on the same phone. Appeal instead. Ban evasion is explicitly prohibited on TikTok and YouTube.

## 7. Paid-promotion and branded-content disclosure rules

Campaign clips are paid promotion. You're paid per view to promote a creator's show, channel or product, and often the sponsor product inside the clip too. Disclose on **every** campaign clip unless the campaign brief explicitly says otherwise, and ask them why if it does.

### TikTok: "Content disclosure and ads" → "Disclose commercial content"
- **Rule:** TikTok's Branded Content Policy defines branded content as content that "promotes or reviews a third-party brand or its products or services in exchange for payment or any other incentive". Creators must "enable the commercial content disclosure toggle" ([TikTok Branded Content Policy](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy/en), published 2026-08-04, effective 2026-08-31, accessed 2026-10-26). It also lists prohibited industries (adult, tobacco/nicotine, weapons and others). Check that a campaign isn't in one.
- **How:** on the post screen → **Content disclosure and ads** (or **More options → Content disclosure and ads**) → turn on **Disclose commercial content** → choose **Your brand** or **Branded content** (pick **Branded content** for third-party campaigns). The post then shows a **"Paid partnership"** or **"Commercial content"** label. You can also add it to an existing post later ([TikTok how-to](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-turn-on-the-commercial-content-disclosure-setting-in-tiktok), undated; [content disclosure setting](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-the-content-disclosure-setting-for-creators), updated April 2026 **⚠ possibly stale**).
- **Consequence if you skip it:** a 24-hour notice, then the video loses FYF eligibility. TikTok says disclosing does not reduce reach (same page).
- Third-party claim: branded posts can't be set to Private ([Creatable](https://support.creatable.io/tiktok-branded-content-disclosure), undated, unverified).

### YouTube: "Paid promotion" checkbox
- **Rule:** if your video features "branded content, sponsorships, endorsements, or other commercial relationships", tell YouTube by ticking the paid promotion box. This adds a disclosure label at the start of the video. "You and the partners you work with are also responsible" for legal compliance (FTC and so on) ([YouTube Help 154235](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235?hl=en), undated, accessed 2026-10-26).
- **How (desktop Studio):** Upload → **Show more** → **Paid promotion** → "Yes, my video includes branded content". For an existing video: Content → the video → tick the box.
- **Phone:** the paid promotion label works for Shorts on mobile and desktop ([Lindsey Gamble](https://lindseygamble.com/blog/youtube-makes-paid-promotion-label-available-on-both-mobile-and-desktop), dated "Nov 4", year not shown, **⚠ possibly stale**). In the YouTube app it's under the upload details / "more options" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/how-to/how-to-upload-youtube-shorts), undated). If you can't find it on your phone, open YouTube Studio on desktop afterwards and tick it there.

### Instagram: "Paid partnership" label
- **Rule:** branded content must use the Paid partnership label. It's available to professional (Creator or Business) accounts in good standing, added only in the Instagram app, and enabled at **Profile → menu → Account → Branded content** ([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/ipad-app/1123581461537025), undated, page text only partly readable; eligibility details from [Flick](https://www.flick.social/learn/blog/post/instagram-branded-content-tools) and [Shane Barker](https://shanebarker.com/blog/instagram-branded-content-tool/), 2026, third-party).
- **How:** while posting a Reel → **Advanced settings / Tag** → **Add paid partnership label** → tag the brand partner (the partner needs a professional account and must approve). If the campaign owner won't be tagged, still turn the label on and add "#ad" text.

### X: "Paid Partnership" content disclosure
- **Rule:** in March 2026 X launched a **Paid Partnership** label under "Content Disclosures", via a **flag icon in the post composer**, or for older posts **post menu → "Add content disclosure"**. X's paid partnerships policy covers promoting third-party products or services for money, in-kind benefits, affiliate commission and similar. Coverage conflicts on whether the label alone is enough, or whether posts also need clear "Ad" wording ([Storyboard18](https://storyboard18.com/advertising/x-introduces-built-in-paid-partnership-label-to-replace-ad-hashtags-91161.htm), [Roboin](https://roboin.io/article/en/2026/03/03/x-adds-ai-and-paid-partnership-labels-to-boost-content-transparency/), [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/x-launches-paid-partnership-labels-to-force-influencers-to-disclose-sponsored-content/), all 2026-03, **⚠ possibly stale**). The official [X Paid Partnerships Policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/paid-partnerships-policy) page returned HTTP 403 to my fetch, so it's unverified. **Do both:** turn on the label and start the post with "Ad:" or "#ad".

### FTC (US law, applies on every platform)
- **Platform tools may not be enough.** The FTC says using a platform's disclosure tool doesn't guarantee a clear and conspicuous disclosure, and "the ultimate responsibility… rests with the influencer and the brand – not the platform" ([FTC, "FTC's Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking"](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking), Guides revised 2023, accessed 2026-10-26, **⚠ possibly stale** by date, but still the current FTC guidance).
- **Put it in the video.** A disclosure in the description alone isn't enough. If the endorsement is visual, the disclosure should be visual; if it's audible, it should be audible; place it near the start. **For short videos like TikTok, superimpose larger, readable text** on the video, because description text is usually too small (same FAQ).
- **Wording that works:** "Ad:", "#ad", "Paid ad", "Advertisement", or "Sponsored by [X]" at the **start**. Avoid burying it in a hashtag pile or putting it only in your bio or profile (same FAQ).
- **Pay of any kind counts.** A "material connection" includes anything of value ([FTC, Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/11/disclosures-101-new-ftc-resources-social-media-influencers), 2019-11, **⚠ possibly stale**). Per-view pay isn't named specifically in the FAQ, but it's compensation. Treat it as a material connection.
- **Is a pure clip an "endorsement"?** It's grey. A clip of a podcaster talking is arguably not *your* endorsement. But you're paid to spread it, and the platform rules (TikTok's "in exchange for payment or any other incentive", X's "monetary payment") clearly cover it. The cheap, safe move is to always disclose. *(This is not legal advice.)*

**Your disclosure checklist for every campaign clip:**
1. Platform toggle on (TikTok "Disclose commercial content → Branded content", YouTube "Paid promotion", Instagram "Paid partnership", X "Paid Partnership").
2. On-screen text in the first 2–3 seconds, readable size: **"#ad – paid clip for @Creator"**, or whatever wording the campaign brief specifies.
3. Caption starts with "#ad" or "Ad:".
4. Keep a screenshot or record of the campaign brief and permission for every clip you post.
