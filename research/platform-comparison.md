# Clipping Platform Comparison

Research date: 2026-10-26. All URLs accessed 2026-10-26 unless noted. "⚠ possibly stale" = published before 2026-04-26.

**Source-quality warning:** most detailed comparisons online are written by **competing platforms** (FindClout, ClipAffiliates) or **clipping-tool vendors** (ClipSpeed, OpusClip, OpenClip). I used primary sources (platform sites, docs, terms) where I could reach them. **Clipping.net returned HTTP 429 (rate-limited / bot wall) to every direct request**, so its figures come from search-engine snippets of clipping.net pages plus FindClout's summary of those pages. Confirm them in the Clipping.net dashboard or Discord.

## TL;DR

- **Start on Clipping.net (already joined) and add Whop Content Rewards as a second source of campaigns.** Clipping.net has live campaigns in your exact niches: gaming (Doublelift) and podcasts (Caleb Hammer). It pays by PayPal with no fees advertised, and has no documented pattern of not paying. Whop has the most campaigns and the clearest official docs and terms.
- **Rates are similar everywhere: about $0.30–$3 per 1K views ($30–$300 per 100K), with ~$1/1K typical.** The differences that matter are minimum-view thresholds, per-clip caps, payout timing, and whether the budget runs out before you're paid.
- **Clipping.net's biggest weakness is payout timing.** You get paid only when the sponsor closes a "cycle", which can take weeks or months. There is no withdraw button, and the dashboard balance is only an estimate.
- **Whop's biggest weaknesses:** fixed budgets paid out first-come-first-served, brands can reject clips, and Trustpilot has **removed Whop's rating for guideline breaches (fake reviews)**. Recent reviews there complain of frozen balances. The Whop fee is disputed: **Whop's official terms say the brand (Seller) pays the 10% fee**, while third-party guides say it comes out of clippers' pay.
- **Avoid Vyro, Reach.cat and ClipAffiliates as a first platform for now.** Vyro is legitimate (MrBeast/ViewStats), but all 7 of its Trustpilot reviews are 1-star "fake views" rejection/locked-earnings complaints. Reach.cat pays only in USDC crypto and has thin coverage. ClipAffiliates reportedly takes 9% from clippers and has very few reviews.

### Notes on the "others"

- **Clipify:** I found **no platform by this name** that pays clippers per view. It may be confused with **Clipency** (Product Hunt: 700+ clippers, $3,758+ paid out, which is tiny) ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/@ayushbera)). Unverified.
- **FindClout:** claims 0% creator commission and 1–3 day payouts ([L]). It's a competitor that writes most of the comparison articles online, so treat its claims as marketing.
- **Clipster** (2–3 week payout claim) and **Promote.fun** (USDC on Solana only): too little information to rank ([L]).
- **ContentRewards.com** shows up as a "discover/clipping" listing site ([contentrewards.com](https://contentrewards.com/c/discover/clipping?page=2)). Not researched further.
- **Direct streamer programs** (e.g. N3on at $40–$50/100K, MrBeast via Vyro): these run through the same platforms or Discords ([Tubefilter, 2026-04-29](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/29/n3on-spending-millions-stream-clippers-tiktok-kick/amp/)).

## Gambling exclusion

Gambling/Kick campaigns pay the most (reportedly $10+/1K) but carry the highest ban and suppression risk ([OpenClip Kick guide](https://openclip.app/guides/clipping-on-kick)). A search of clipping.net turned up a campaign labelled **"Stake"** (a crypto casino; [Forbes, 2026-04-26](https://www.forbes.com/sites/boazsobrado/2026/04/26/the-creator-of-clipping-who-powers-stakes-viral-machine/) profiles Stake's clipping machine). **ClipForge needs a filter or blocklist for campaign names and brands** (Stake, Roobet, Shuffle, Rollbit, Gamdom, "casino", "slots", "bet") on every platform.

## Compliance note (all platforms)

Vyro lists **missing ad disclosure** as a common rejection reason ([vyro.com](https://www.vyro.com/)), and one guide notes that most clipping campaigns lack the paid-promotion disclosures the FTC technically requires ([summary of ssemble/OpenClip, 2026](https://www.ssemble.com/blog/best-clipping-platforms-2026)). Since you're a US-based clipper, **turn on the TikTok/IG/YouTube "paid partnership" toggle or add #ad** unless a campaign brief says otherwise. Check each brief.

## Recommendation

**1. Primary: Clipping.net (already joined).**
- It has campaigns in all three target niches: gaming (Doublelift), podcasts (Caleb Hammer), and creator/streamer content ([B]). A US clipper on the Caleb Hammer campaign told the BBC he makes about $1,000/month ([BBC via Ghanamma](https://www.ghanamma.com/?p=180503) ⚠).
- PayPal payouts with no fees advertised ([A]), and no reports of non-payment found.
- What to do: pick campaigns with **low minimums (≤25K total, 1K per post)** and **$40+/100K**. Never delete or archive a clip before the cycle closes ([A]). Screenshot view counts yourself, because final payouts can land below the dashboard estimate.

**2. Secondary: Whop Content Rewards (sign up this week).**
- It's the largest marketplace, with clear official docs and terms ([C][J]), many podcast and creator campaigns, and **next-day ACH for $2.50** ([M]).
- It fills Clipping.net's gaps. Payment is triggered by approval rather than open-ended sponsor cycles, so you get paid sooner (per third-party reports).
- What to do: submit early in a campaign's life (budgets are first-come-first-served). Read each brief's required tags and links (the most common rejection reason). Withdraw often rather than letting a balance build up (because of the frozen-funds complaints on Trustpilot [P]).

**Skip for now:** Vyro (funds locked until the campaign ends, plus fake-view flag complaints [Q]), Reach.cat (crypto-only, thin track record), and ClipAffiliates (9% cut, tiny sample). Look again after 60 days of data.
