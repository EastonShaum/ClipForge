# Account handle ideas

Researched 2026-10-26. Availability was checked on **2026-10-26** by fetching each profile URL. **⚠ possibly stale** means a source was published before 2026-04-26. **Folk wisdom** means unconfirmed advice.

## TL;DR

- **Top 5 picks:** `highlightmill` (works for any campaign), `ggcutroom` (gaming), `sitdownsnips` (podcasts/interviews), `eastoncuts` (personal brand), `forgeclipsco` (matches the ClipForge business name). All five were **free on YouTube** and showed **no Instagram profile** on 2026-10-26. TikTok and X are **unverified**, so check them in the apps today.
- **The exact "ClipForge" name is taken.** `@clipforge`, `@clipforgehq` and `@theclipforge` are taken on YouTube, and `clipforge` is taken on Instagram. Using it publicly would risk confusion with someone else's account.
- **Keep handles at 15 characters or less, letters and numbers only** (no dots or underscores), so the same handle fits X, TikTok, YouTube and Instagram.
- **Never put the clipped creator's name in your handle** or copy their photo or branding. YouTube bans "Official", "Backup" or "Shorts" variants of a creator's name. X requires "fan" or "commentary" at the start of the display name for accounts about someone. Instagram removes impersonators.
- Choose a **creator-agnostic** name for your main account, because clipping work moves between many creators. Save niche names for a possible second account later.

## 1. Naming principles

1. **Memorable and sayable.** Two short words, no number strings, no "official" (folk wisdom, but it also matters for the impersonation rules below).
2. **Signals the niche or the job** ("cut", "clip", "snips", "gg", "mic", "sitdown") so the test audience and campaign managers get it instantly.
3. **Fits every platform.** Usernames run 3–30 characters on YouTube, 4–15 on X, and up to 24 on TikTok. X allows only letters, numbers and underscores; Instagram and TikTok also allow periods ([HandleGrab cross-platform rules](https://www.handlegrab.com/social-media-username-rules), undated, third-party; check in each app). **So: 15 characters or less, letters and numbers only.**
4. **Creator-agnostic beats creator-specific** for a clipper. One account can carry many campaigns, and a creator's name in your handle is an impersonation risk.
5. **Never impersonate the creator you clip.**
   - **YouTube:** bans "unauthorized impersonation of a person, entity, or channel", copying another creator's profile picture, banner or color scheme, using "Real" or "Official" to pass as them, and **backup-style names that add "Backup", "Shorts" or "Live" to a creator's name**. Fan channels must "state so explicitly in your channel name or handle" ([YouTube Help, impersonation policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801947?hl=en), undated, accessed 2026-10-26).
   - **TikTok:** lists impersonation as prohibited deceptive behavior. Multiple accounts are allowed "such as for fan content" but not to deceive ([TikTok CG, Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity), effective 2026-09-24).
   - **Instagram:** impersonation "could result in the removal of your account". Accounts dedicated to a public figure may be asked to make clear they aren't affiliated, for example by labeling themselves a fan or commentary account ([Instagram Help](https://help.Instagram.com/1140918252762216), undated, from search snippet; direct fetch returned no text).
   - **X:** since April 2025, parody, commentary and fan accounts must put a keyword such as "fan" or "commentary" **at the start of the display name** and use a different avatar from the person's ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/04/223-x-parody-account-rules-2025/), 2025-04, **⚠ possibly stale**; X's help page blocked fetch). A creator-agnostic clip account isn't "about" one person, but if you ever run a single-creator page on X, follow this.
6. **Don't collide with an existing brand.** Several "ClipForge" / "Clip Forge" accounts already exist (see §3).

## 2. Candidates with availability

**How I checked:**
- **YouTube:** `youtube.com/@handle` returning **HTTP 404** = handle free. A page titled with a channel name = **taken**. I tested with a nonsense handle to confirm 404 means free. YouTube can still refuse a free handle for its own reasons, so claim it to be sure.
- **Instagram:** if a profile name or follower count came back = **taken**. A bare "Instagram" page (the same thing a made-up nonsense handle returns) = **"no profile found (likely free, unverified)"**, because a login wall could also look like this.
- **TikTok:** every fetch, including a famous account (`@khaby.lame`), returned only "TikTok - Make Your Day". **Unverified for all.**
- **X:** fetches returned HTTP 402/403. **Unverified for all.** I didn't use workarounds to get past bot checks.

## 4. Recommendation

1. **Main account (all platforms): `highlightmill`.** It's creator-agnostic and niche-agnostic, so every gaming *and* podcast campaign fits. Alternative: `forgeclipsco` if you want the ClipForge tie-in.
2. **If you'd rather be a face:** `eastoncuts`.
3. **Later second accounts, only once the main one works:** `ggcutroom` (gaming) and `sitdownsnips` (podcasts). Keep their content different from the main account (see `account-ramp-up.md` §5).
4. **Today, before anyone else does:** in each app, try to claim your pick on **TikTok and X first**, since those are the unverified ones, then YouTube and Instagram. If TikTok or X is taken, fall back to the next name in the same group, so the handle stays identical across platforms.
5. **Display name:** something like "Highlight Mill | Gaming & Podcast Clips". Put "Clips with creator permission" in the bio. Never use the creator's name or photo as your display name or avatar.
